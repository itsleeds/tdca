What are Data Science and AI?

Data science: turns raw data into understanding and insight.

AI: generative language models to generate code and other outputs for decision-making.

  • Machine Learning: pattern recognition, prediction, and optimisation — from traffic forecasting to demand modelling.
  • In transport, this means handling diverse data sources on transport infrastructure, behaviour, and flows:
    • There is more data than ever before.
    • Example: OpenStreetMap data available worldwide (Boeing 2017).

AI for Transport Planning

  • AI is transforming transport (traffic prediction, safety analysis), but value is unlocked only through reproducible workflows.
Key Advantage How Reproducibility Enables This Impact on AI & Planning
Auditability & Trust Version control (Git) logs every step, dataset, and random seed. Prevents black-box results; ensures public policy decisions are auditable.
AI-Assisted Coding Workflows are text-based scripts (Python/R) rather than hidden GUI menus. AI assistants can directly write, debug, and explain code in your editor.
Agentic Loops Execution pipelines are programmatic, automated, and self-contained. AI agents can run automated loops to optimise parameters and test models.
Robust Stability Package managers (Conda/Pip) pin dependencies and library versions. Combats the non-deterministic nature of AI to ensure consistent results.

Key insight: AI works best when the environment is defined in code, so every iteration is captured, auditable, and repeatable.

AI in Action: JamCam Lab & Computer Vision

  • Real-world AI project: JamCam Lab (tfl_yolo), developed by Dr Christopher Rushton (ITS Leeds).
  • Uses YOLO object detection on ~890 live Transport for London (TfL) roadside camera feeds.
  • Automated counting and classification of vehicles, cyclists, and pedestrians.
  • Demonstrates why AI outputs must be paired with camera calibration, perspective correction, validation, and domain expertise.

Core planning insight: “Getting counts out of a traffic camera is easy; getting counts you would defend in public is the whole course.”

The AI Paradox: Upsides and “AI Surrender”

Great Potential Upsides

  • Rapid Prototyping: Instant generation of boilerplate code, data wrangling pipelines, and plotting syntax.
  • Error Diagnostics: Fast explanations for obscure stack traces and package dependency errors.
  • Exploratory Breadth: Quickly learning new libraries (e.g., GeoPandas, Shapely, PyTorch, Quarto).

The Risk of “AI Surrender”

  • Cognitive Surrender: Documented by the MIT Committee on AI in Education (2026).
  • Loss of Productive Struggle: Outsourcing cognitive effort at the first sign of difficulty.
  • The “Illusion of Learning”: Fast correct answers create a false sense of mastery without mental encoding.
  • Social Erosion: Marked drops in study group collaboration and office hour attendance.

Empirical Evidence: Homework vs. Exams

  • The Generative AI Penalty: Randomised trial by Bastani et al. (2025) in PNAS:
    • Practice scores rose +48% with standard AI (“GPT Base”).
    • On unassisted exams, those same students scored 17% worse than students who never used AI.
  • International Evidence (26,000+ students): Unrestricted AI boosts homework (+18%) while closed-book exam scores drop (−20%).
  • The Remedy: Guardrails and friction (“GPT Tutor”) that prompt thinking rather than copy-paste solutions.

Why Reproducibility Matters

  • Reproducibility: Others (or future you) can run your code and get the exact same results.
  • Excel vs. Code:
    • Excel: Steps are hidden behind point-and-click menus; difficult to audit, version, or scale.
    • Code: Every transformation is explicitly documented, version-controlled, and repeatable.
  • AI + Reproducibility: AI assistants help write reproducible pipelines faster, but the pipeline must remain transparent and scripted to be verified.
  • Transparency: Crucial for public policy and planning — AI-driven decisions in transport affect people’s lives.

The Power of (AI-Powered) Programming

  • Flexibility: You are not limited by what a GUI dropdown menu allows.
  • Scalability: The same script can process 10 rows or 10 million rows.
  • Sharing & Collaboration: Open-source packages mean you stand on the shoulders of giants.
  • Automation: Spend more time thinking and designing solutions, less time copy-pasting.
  • AI as an Accelerator: Assistants generate boilerplate and explain errors, but understanding the code you run is still essential.
  • The Learning Curve: Programming has a steeper start than point-and-click tools. The Python tutorials in this module guide you step-by-step.

Tools of the Trade

  • Python: A first-class citizen in data science and AI. Versatile and powerful.
  • R: Excellent for statistics and spatial analysis (Arribas-Bel 2019).
  • Quarto: The multi-language publishing tool — mixes code, text, and output; renders to slides, PDF, HTML (Allaire et al. 2024).
  • AI coding assistants: Tools like GitHub Copilot work directly in your editor, helping you write, understand, and debug code faster.
  • The ecosystem: Python/R for analysis, Quarto for communication, AI assistants for productivity.

Interactive Visualisations

  • Made possible with code (e.g., Python’s Streamlit, Plotly, or R’s Shiny).
  • Impact: Stakeholders can “play” with the data.
  • Examples:
    • Propensity to Cycle Tool (PCT)
    • Interactive road safety maps.
  • Increases the transparency and reach of research.

Making the Most of Transport Data

  • Diverse Data Sources: Smart card taps, GPS trajectories, traffic counts, and crowd-sourced feeds.
  • Where the value lies: Not in raw datasets, but in your processing pipeline:
    • Clean & Validate: Handling missing values, sensor failures, and anomalies.
    • Visualise: Exposing spatial patterns, bottlenecks, and travel trends.
    • Model: Predicting demand and optimising traffic flow.
  • How AI Enhances This: Automating anomaly detection, clustering travel patterns, and forecasting future demand.
  • Foundation for the Future: TDCA builds the core skills for working with transport data — scaling directly into Transport Data Science (TDS).
